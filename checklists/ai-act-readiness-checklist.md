# AI Act Readiness Checklist

Use this as a practical readiness checklist for organizations that develop, deploy, buy, or invest in AI systems that may touch the EU market.

This is not legal advice. The AI Act is regulatory work, not a vibes-based spreadsheet. Verify classification, obligations, timelines, and national implementation with qualified counsel.

## Current Official Timeline Snapshot

As of June 2026, official EU materials state:

- Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 entered into force on 1 August 2024.
- Prohibited AI practices and AI literacy obligations applied from 2 February 2025.
- Governance rules and general-purpose AI model obligations applied from 2 August 2025.
- The AI Act is broadly applicable from 2 August 2026, with exceptions.
- Official EU materials note an updated transition period for some high-risk AI systems embedded in regulated products following the AI omnibus process.

Primary sources:

- European Commission AI Act overview: https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/regulatory-framework-ai
- European Commission AI Act FAQ: https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/navigating-ai-act
- Regulation text: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj

## 1. Scope

- [ ] The organization knows which AI systems it develops, deploys, distributes, imports, or uses.
- [ ] AI systems are mapped to EU users, customers, employees, or markets.
- [ ] Roles are identified: provider, deployer, importer, distributor, product manufacturer, authorized representative, or model provider.
- [ ] General-purpose AI model use or provision is identified.
- [ ] Open-source dependencies are assessed where relevant.
- [ ] Contractual obligations with vendors and customers are reviewed.

## 2. Prohibited AI Practices

- [ ] The organization has screened systems against prohibited practices.
- [ ] The review includes manipulation, exploitation of vulnerabilities, social scoring, certain biometric categorization, certain emotion recognition, and other prohibited categories.
- [ ] Product, sales, and customer success teams know which use cases cannot be accepted.
- [ ] There is a process to reject customer requests that would move a system into prohibited use.

**Block if**

- A system appears to fall into a prohibited category and there is no formal legal review.

## 3. AI Literacy

- [ ] Staff using or supervising AI systems receive role-appropriate training.
- [ ] Training covers limitations, appropriate use, escalation, data handling, and human oversight.
- [ ] Training is documented.
- [ ] High-impact teams receive deeper training than casual users.

## 4. Classification

- [ ] Each AI system is classified as prohibited, high-risk, transparency-obligation, GPAI-related, limited risk, or minimal risk.
- [ ] Classification reasoning is documented.
- [ ] Annex III and product-safety linkage are reviewed where applicable.
- [ ] Classification is reviewed when use case, market, user group, autonomy, or integration changes.
- [ ] Legal counsel reviews borderline or high-impact systems.

## 5. High-Risk System Readiness

For systems that may be high-risk:

- [ ] Risk management system exists.
- [ ] Data governance and data quality controls exist.
- [ ] Technical documentation is prepared.
- [ ] Logging capabilities exist.
- [ ] Transparency and instructions for use are prepared.
- [ ] Human oversight is designed and tested.
- [ ] Accuracy, robustness, and cybersecurity are evaluated.
- [ ] Quality management system exists where provider obligations apply.
- [ ] Post-market monitoring process exists.
- [ ] Serious incident reporting process exists.
- [ ] Conformity assessment path is identified.

## 6. General-Purpose AI Models

For organizations providing or substantially modifying GPAI models:

- [ ] GPAI model status is assessed against current Commission guidance.
- [ ] Technical documentation obligations are assessed.
- [ ] Copyright policy obligations are assessed.
- [ ] Training-content summary obligations are assessed.
- [ ] Systemic-risk status is assessed where relevant.
- [ ] Risk assessment, mitigation, incident reporting, and cybersecurity obligations are assessed for systemic-risk models.
- [ ] Open-source-specific treatment is reviewed with counsel.

## 7. Transparency Obligations

- [ ] Users are informed when interacting with AI where required.
- [ ] AI-generated or manipulated content is labeled where required.
- [ ] Emotion recognition or biometric categorization notices are handled where relevant.
- [ ] Deepfake and synthetic content workflows are reviewed.
- [ ] User notices are understandable, not buried in generic terms.

## 8. Technical Documentation

- [ ] System purpose and intended use are documented.
- [ ] Architecture and model information are documented.
- [ ] Data sources and data governance are documented.
- [ ] Evaluation methodology and results are documented.
- [ ] Risk controls are documented.
- [ ] Human oversight design is documented.
- [ ] Logging and monitoring are documented.
- [ ] Vendor and subprocessor dependencies are documented.

## 9. Operating Model

- [ ] AI Act accountability owner is named.
- [ ] Product, engineering, legal, compliance, security, and procurement responsibilities are clear.
- [ ] Approval gates exist for new AI systems.
- [ ] Procurement process includes AI Act classification.
- [ ] Customer-request review process exists.
- [ ] Evidence retention process exists.
- [ ] Incident process connects technical and legal workflows.

## Practical Advice

Start with inventory and classification. If those are weak, everything downstream is theater.
